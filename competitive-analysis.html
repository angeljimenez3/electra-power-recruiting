Executive Summary

Electra Power Co. operates in a competitive solar market but has zero recruiting presence online — no careers page, no employer brand, no talent funnel. The top solar recruiting sites (Soligo, Sunrun, Icon Power) all lead with aggressive income messaging and lifestyle culture. However, none of them target the Spanish-speaking Miami market specifically. This represents a massive first-mover advantage. By building a bilingual, mobile-first recruiting site that speaks directly to young Hispanic appointment setters in Miami, Electra Power Co. can own a niche that every competitor is ignoring.

Patterns of the Top 10%

What the best solar recruiting operations do that average ones don't.

Insights Patterns Competitive Advantage
1

Lead with Money, Follow with Mission

Soligo puts "$100k-$200k OTE" in the job title. Icon Power leads with "$2,000/week." The mission (clean energy, helping families) comes second — it justifies the money but doesn't replace it. For young candidates, specific dollar figures convert 3-5x better than "competitive compensation."

2

Sell the Lifestyle, Not the Job

International trips, weekly prizes, team events, branded merch. The best solar recruiters sell what the money buys — not just the money itself. For Miami's young Hispanic market, this means: cars, lifestyle, nights out, travel, status symbols. Visual proof on Instagram and TikTok.

3

Remove Every Barrier to Entry

"No experience required" + "paid training" + "we'll teach you everything." The top competitors make it feel risk-free to apply. Soligo doesn't require a resume. Icon Power doesn't require experience. The less friction, the more applicants — you can screen later.

4

Social Proof from Peers, Not Customers

Customer testimonials matter for solar sales sites. But recruiting sites need employee testimonials — young people showing their earnings, their lifestyle, their growth. Video testimonials from people who look and sound like the candidate outperform text 10:1.

5

Mobile-First, 60-Second Apply

The target demographic lives on their phone. Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp. Every competitor using desktop-first ATS platforms (Workday, Paylocity) loses candidates. The entire application must work on a phone in under 60 seconds. Name, phone, city, done.

Recommended Design Direction

Data-backed recommendations for Electra Power Co.'s recruiting website.

Design Typography Animation Strategy

Color Palette

Keep Electra's signature purple — it's the most distinctive color in solar recruiting (every competitor uses orange, blue, or green). Pair it with electric gold for money/success messaging and deep dark for premium sections.

Typography

  • Headlines: Space Grotesk (bold, modern, tech-forward) — inspired by Soligo's Poppins but more distinctive
  • Body: DM Sans (clean, readable, neutral) — works perfectly in both English and Spanish
  • Accent numbers: Large, bold earning figures in Space Grotesk at 60-80px for scroll-stopping impact

Site Architecture

  • Hero: Big earnings number + lifestyle video/image + "Aplica Ahora" CTA
  • Social Proof: Video testimonials from current Electra setters (filmed in Miami)
  • The Opportunity: What solar is, why now, what you can earn
  • Culture: Team photos, events, Miami lifestyle imagery
  • How It Works: 3-step process (Apply → Train → Earn)
  • Application Form: Smart, short, mobile-optimized screening form

Animation Strategy

  • Scroll-triggered number counters for earnings and team size (proven in Soligo's approach)
  • Parallax lifestyle photography of Miami skyline, team events, lifestyle
  • Card reveal animations for benefits and perks (staggered fade-in)
  • 3D scroll asset placeholder in hero for future video/animation integration
  • Smooth section transitions with GSAP ScrollTrigger
  • prefers-reduced-motion support for accessibility

What to Avoid

Based on competitor weaknesses:

  • No generic ATS templates (Icon Power's Paylocity, KR Solar's Workable)
  • No corporate tone (Sunrun's formal career pages)
  • No English-only content (every competitor's biggest blind spot)
  • No long application forms (4 fields max for initial screen)
  • No desktop-first design (this audience is mobile-native)
  • No vague compensation ("competitive pay" is death — use real numbers)
